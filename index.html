Use MaxChess in ChatGPT 🤖♟️

Meet the MaxChess companion: a chess helper in ChatGPT. Ask questions, share a position or paste a game to learn more about chess.

What can you use it for?

  • Chess coaching: understand tactics, strategy, openings and endgames.
  • Game reviews: share a PGN or move list and learn from mistakes and missed chances.
  • Position help: share a FEN or describe a board position to explore candidate moves and threats.
  • Puzzle explanations: understand why a tactic works, not just which move to play.
  • Training plans: build a practice routine, lesson or chess-club activity.
  • Lichess help: get guidance with public profiles, studies and importing or exporting games.

What it does not do

The ChatGPT companion gives advice and analysis in the chat. It does not automatically log in to your MaxChess, Lichess or Chess.com accounts, change ratings, or publish games. For playing on the actual MaxChess board, use the free website.

The pieces

👑 A lesson from the MaxChess owner

MADE BY MAXCHESS OWNER

Build a strong opening

In the opening, try to control the centre with pawns, develop your knights and bishops, and castle to keep your king safe. Avoid moving the same piece again and again when you could develop another piece.

Remember: Centre, develop, castle.

Quick challenge: Name three useful opening goals.

Answer: Control the centre, develop your pieces, and make your king safe by castling.

Explore all learning tools

Your lessons, opening study, practice room and daily training are all part of Learn.

🎯 Put it into practice

After each lesson, try a puzzle to use what you learned.

Opening principles

1. Fight for the center. Move a central pawn to claim space.

2. Develop your minor pieces. Bring knights and bishops into the game.

3. Keep your king safe. Castle early when it is sensible.

4. Don't bring your queen out too early. Develop your pieces before launching an attack.

Practice room

Small habits make a stronger player.

Before each move, ask yourself:

  1. Is my king safe?
  2. Can I capture something for free?
  3. What is my opponent threatening?
  4. Will my piece be safe on the new square?

How to improve faster

What should I do before every move?

Check for checks, captures and threats for both sides. Ask what your opponent wants to do next.

How much should I practice?

Try 15–25 focused minutes. Stop when you are tired and return later.

How do I review a game?

Look for the first moment the position changed in your opponent's favor. Find a safer or more active move.

Does this change my official online rating?

No. Training points and streaks are personal progress saved in this browser. They are not a ranked chess rating.

THE MAXCHESS STORY

📖 The Creation of MaxChess

A story about building a free place to play, learn and enjoy chess.

Chapter 1 — The idea

MaxChess began with a simple goal: make chess fun and easy to access. The idea was to create a free chess website where people could play, practise, learn and enjoy the game without needing a paid subscription.

It was meant to feel welcoming to beginners and interesting for players who already knew the game.

Chapter 2 — Building a place to play

The project grew around the chessboard. Players should be able to play against the computer, challenge other people, use clocks and keep improving. A good chess site also needs clear controls that work on computers, tablets and phones.

Chapter 3 — Learning the game

MaxChess grew beyond playing games. Lessons, puzzles and next-move challenges help players understand why a move is strong, not just which move to play. The lesson board lets a writer set up positions by placing and moving pieces.

Chapter 4 — A community project

MaxChess is designed to welcome contributions from chess fans: puzzle makers, coaches, lesson writers, designers, testers, translators, event hosts and more. Community contributions should be original, kind and reviewed with care.

Lessons made by contributors can be identified as MADE BY MAXCHESS USERS, while owner-created lessons have their own label.

Chapter 5 — What comes next

The project keeps growing through ideas, testing and improvements. The aim is to make MaxChess more useful, reliable and enjoyable while keeping the core experience free.

Every good suggestion, carefully tested feature and helpful lesson can become part of the next chapter.

Written for the MaxChess website. This first edition describes the project and its goals; it does not invent exact dates, private conversations or events that have not been documented.
